# Quotations, Alterations & Omissions - Bluebook Rule 5

Complete guidance for block quotations, bracketed alterations, and ellipses per Bluebook 21st Edition.

## The Iron Law of Omissions

<EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>
**NEVER BEGIN A QUOTATION WITH AN ELLIPSIS.**

An omission at the *start* of quoted language is shown by bracketed capitalization, or by
lowercasing the phrase into your own sentence — never by leading dots.

```
WRONG:   ". . . borders are less of a barrier to economic exchange."
RIGHT:   "[B]orders are less of a barrier to economic exchange."
RIGHT:   The court found "borders" to be "less of a barrier to economic exchange."
```

This is the Rule 5 error editors catch most often. A leading ellipsis marks you as someone who
has not read Rule 5.3(b)(i).
</EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>

## Formatting of Quotations (Rule 5.1)

### The Fifty-Word Threshold

| Length | Format |
|--------|--------|
| 49 words or fewer | Run into the text, enclosed in quotation marks |
| 50 words or more | Block quotation — indented both sides, single spaced, justified, **no quotation marks** |

**Counting words:** hyphenated words count as one. Words added in brackets count. Omitted words
and the ellipses replacing them do not count.

### Block Quotation Form

- Indented left **and** right; single spaced; justified.
- **No enclosing quotation marks.** The indentation is the quotation mark.
- Quotation marks *inside* the original are retained as they appear — double, not single.
- The citation is **not** indented: it begins at the left margin on the line following the block
  (or goes in a footnote).
- The footnote call number goes after the block's final punctuation, not inside the block.

```
    [T]his presumptive privilege must be considered in light of our historic commitment
    to the rule of law. This is nowhere more profoundly manifest than in our view that
    "the twofold aim [of criminal justice] is that guilt shall not escape or innocence
    suffer." We have elected to employ an adversary system of criminal justice in which
    the parties contest all issues before a court of law . . . .

United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683, 708-09 (1974) (citation omitted).
```

### Introducing a Block Quote

Use a complete clause with a colon, or an incomplete clause with no punctuation. Never an
incomplete clause with a colon.

```
RIGHT:   The Court stated:              [complete clause + colon]
RIGHT:   The Court stated,
RIGHT:   The Court stated that          [incomplete clause, no punctuation]
WRONG:   The Court stated that:
```

After "The Court stated that" the first word of the block is **not** capitalized.

### Paragraph Structure Inside a Block Quote

| Situation | Signal |
|-----------|--------|
| Quote begins at the start of a source paragraph | Indent the first line further |
| Quote begins mid-paragraph | **No ellipsis, and do not indent the first line** (p. 103) |
| Second paragraph immediately follows the first in the original | Indent identically, no ellipsis |
| Text existed between the two quoted paragraphs | Prefix the second paragraph with a three-period ellipsis |
| An entire paragraph is omitted inside the quote | Four-period ellipsis **on its own indented line** |

### Where Block Format Does NOT Apply

- Inside an explanatory parenthetical — a 50+ word quotation in a parenthetical stays inline in
  quotation marks, however long it is.
- Block format **does** apply to a 50+ word quotation in ordinary footnote text.

### Quotation Marks and Punctuation (Rule 5.1(b)(iv))

Commas and periods go **inside** the closing quotation mark. Colons, semicolons, question marks,
and exclamation points go **outside** — unless they are part of the quoted passage.

Single quotation marks enclose a quotation within a quotation. When the material quoted consists
*entirely* of a quotation within a quotation, use only one set (double).

## Alterations (Rule 5.2)

Rule 5 lets you bend a quotation to fit your sentence — substitute letters or words, add words,
drop letters, add or drop emphasis, drop citations — provided you **show every change**.
Brackets and parentheticals are how you show it.

### Substituted or Added Letters

```
Original:  Plough deep, while sluggards sleep.
Quoted:    Franklin reminds us to "[p]lough deep, while sluggards sleep."

Original:  Remember, my name is Benjamin.
Quoted:    As Franklin said, "[M]y name is Benjamin."
```

### Omitted Letters — Rule 5.2(b)

Letters dropped from a common root word take **empty brackets**:

```
"The court entered judgment[] for the plaintiff."   (source: "judgments")
```

Verified against the rule text: "Indicate the omission of letters from a common root word with
empty brackets (`judgment[]`)" (5.2(b), p. 104).

### Changed or Added Words

Any word you substitute or insert goes in brackets:

```
"[E]conomic activity continues its relentless drive toward world-wide scope, [so]
trademarks become even more important."

"The defendant appeal[ed] the judgment."
```

### When NOT to Bracket

<EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>
**Brackets mark a change. If nothing changed, brackets are a lie.**

| Do not bracket | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| A letter already in the case you need | Bracket capitalization *only* when the original case differs |
| An unchanged word | Brackets assert an alteration that never happened |
| An omission of words | That is an ellipsis (Rule 5.3), not an alteration |
| A spelling or capitalization convention that merely differs from your journal's | Not an error; leave it |
| Emphasis that was in the original | See "(emphasis in original)" below — it does not exist |
</EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>

The capitalization test: is the quotation a syntactic part of your sentence?

| Quotation is... | Initial letter | Bracket? |
|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| A syntactic part of your sentence | lowercase | only if uppercase in the original |
| Not a syntactic part (stands as its own sentence) | uppercase | only if lowercase in the original |

### [sic]

Insert `[sic]` immediately after an error that appears in the original.

```
The letter stated that the parties "recieved [sic] notice on Tuesday."
```

Use it sparingly — the book's trigger is a **significant** mistake. Where one applies, mark it
and leave it: "Significant mistakes in the original should be followed by `[sic]` and otherwise
left as they appear in the original" (5.2(c), p. 104). Do NOT silently repair the error in
brackets instead; the book's own example leaves a grammatical error standing (`"This list of
statutes are [sic] necessarily incomplete."`) rather than writing `statutes [is]`. Irregular
spelling in historical materials is preserved with no notation unless the reader would otherwise
be confused. Note the typography: *sic* is italicized; the brackets
around it are not.

### Explanatory Parentheticals for Alterations

| Parenthetical | Use |
|---------------|-----|
| `(emphasis added)` | You added the italics |
| `(emphasis omitted)` | You dropped italics that were in the original |
| `(emphasis added and omitted)` | You did both |
| `(alteration in original)` | Brackets or ellipses in the quote came from the source |
| `(citation omitted)` / `(citations omitted)` | You dropped a citation from inside the quote |
| `(footnote omitted)` / `(footnotes omitted)` | You dropped a footnote from **inside** the quote — but NOT one whose call number follows the last word quoted (5.2(d)(ii)) |
| `(internal quotation marks omitted)` | Only "internal" combines with quotation marks — never with citation or footnote |

**`(emphasis in original)` DOES NOT EXIST.** Rule 5.2(d)(iii) directs you not to indicate that
emphasis was in the original. If you see it, delete it.

**Do not combine alteration parentheticals.** Each gets its own:

```
RIGHT:  San Antonio Indep. Sch. Dist. v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 1, 24 (1973)
        (internal quotation marks omitted) (emphasis added).
WRONG:  ... (internal quotation marks omitted and emphasis added).
```

Order follows Rule 1.5(b): `(alteration in original) (emphasis added) (footnote omitted)
(citations omitted) (quoting ...)` then the explanatory parenthetical.

When the quotation sits inside a parenthetical, its alteration parentheticals go inside too:

```
Sorensen, Breaking Bluebook, 88 Va. L. Rev. 320, 322 (2009) ("I am totally crushing this
edit." (emphasis added) (citation omitted)).
```

## Omissions (Rule 5.3)

### The Ellipsis Itself

**Three periods, separated by spaces, and set off by a space on each side.**

```
RIGHT:   locals celebrate Mardi Gras once a year . . . and city workers clean up
WRONG:   locals celebrate Mardi Gras once a year ... and city workers clean up
WRONG:   locals celebrate Mardi Gras once a year … and city workers clean up
```

The single-glyph ellipsis character (…) that word processors autocorrect to is **not** Bluebook
form. In Word, the spaces are typed manually and should be non-breaking (Ctrl+Shift+Space, or
Option+Space on a Mac) so the dots never break across a line — before the first period, between
each period, and after the last.

### Beginning of a Quoted Sentence Omitted — Rule 5.3(b)(i)

No ellipsis. Capitalize the first letter of the quoted language and bracket it if it was not
already capitalized. See the Iron Law above.

### Middle of a Quoted Sentence Omitted — Rule 5.3(b)(ii)

```
"National borders are less of a barrier . . . now than at almost any other time in history."
```

### End of a Quoted Sentence Omitted — Rule 5.3(b)(iii)

Insert the ellipsis **between the last quoted word and the sentence's final punctuation**. The
result is four dots: three for the ellipsis, one for the sentence.

```
"National borders are less of a barrier to economic exchange now than at almost any other
time . . . ."
```

Note the spacing: the word came from the middle of the original sentence, so there **is** a
space before the first period.

### Material After a Complete Quoted Sentence Omitted — Rule 5.3(b)(iv)-(vi)

**(iv)** If nothing follows in your quotation, do **not** mark the deletion at all:

```
"National borders are less of a barrier to economic exchange now than at almost any other
time in history."
```

**(v)** If further quotation follows, retain the original sentence's punctuation and insert the
ellipsis before the remainder. Because the period ends the original sentence, there is **no
space** between it and the first ellipsis period:

```
"... at almost any other time in history. . . . [E]conomic activity continues its
relentless drive toward world-wide scope."
```

**(vi)** If material is omitted both at the end of a sentence **and** after it, use only one
ellipsis for both. The sentence's own punctuation follows the ellipsis:

```
"National borders are less of a barrier . . . . [E]conomic activity continues ..."
"Why, then, are certain scholars advocating less . . . ? [T]his seems counterintuitive."
```

### Spacing: the Four-Dot Rule of Thumb

| The word before the ellipsis is... | Spacing |
|------------------------------------|---------|
| From the middle of the original sentence | space before the dots: `barrier . . . .` |
| The last word of the original sentence (its period is retained) | no space before the dots: `history. . . .` |

### Quoting a Phrase or Clause

When the quoted language is a phrase or clause inside your own sentence rather than a full
sentence, you need not mark omissions **before or after** it — only omissions inside it.

```
The court called the practice "a barrier to economic exchange" that Congress
had "long sought . . . to dismantle."
```

### Omitting a Paragraph in a Block Quote

A four-period ellipsis on its own indented line. See the Rule 5.1 paragraph-structure table above.

## When an Ellipsis Is WRONG

<EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>
| You omitted | Correct signal | NOT |
|-------------|----------------|-----|
| A citation inside the quote | `(citation omitted)` after the cite | ellipsis |
| A footnote inside the quote | `(footnote omitted)` after the cite | ellipsis |
| A citation or footnote call number **following the last word quoted** | **nothing — do not indicate it** (5.2(d)(ii), p. 105) | any parenthetical |
| Internal quotation marks | `(internal quotation marks omitted)` | ellipsis |
| The start of the quoted sentence | bracketed capitalization | ellipsis |
| Matter after the last quoted sentence's final punctuation | nothing at all | ellipsis |
| Nothing — you *changed* a word | brackets | ellipsis |

An ellipsis stands for words the author wrote and you dropped. It never stands for an
apparatus you dropped, and it never stands for a word you rewrote.
</EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>

## Worked Examples

Original source:

> National borders are less of a barrier to economic exchange now than at almost any other time
> in history.<sup>12</sup> As economic activity continues its relentless drive toward world-wide
> scope, trademarks become even more important.

| Before (wrong) | After (right) | Rule |
|----------------|---------------|------|
| `". . . borders are less of a barrier."` | `"[B]orders are less of a barrier."` | 5.3(b)(i) |
| `"National borders are less of a barrier...now than..."` | `"National borders are less of a barrier . . . now than . . . ."` | 5.3 spacing |
| `"...less of a barrier to economic exchange now…"` | `"[L]ess of a barrier to economic exchange now . . . ."` | 5.3(b)(i), (iii) |
| `"...at almost any other time . . ."` | `"...at almost any other time . . . ."` | 5.3(b)(iii) |
| `"...in history . . . . As economic activity..."` | `"...in history. . . . [A]s economic activity..."` | 5.3(b)(v) |
| `"...in history [footnote omitted] . . . ."` | `"...in history."` — **no parenthetical**: the call number follows the last word quoted | 5.2(d)(ii) |
| `"National borders are [now] less of a barrier"` (source said "now") | `"National borders are now less of a barrier"` | 5.2 — no change, no bracket |
| `... (emphasis in original).` | delete the parenthetical | 5.2(d)(iii) |
| `... (citation omitted and emphasis added).` | `... (emphasis added) (citation omitted).` | 5.2(d), 1.5(b) |
| A 62-word quotation in quotation marks, run into the text | Block: indented both sides, no quotation marks, cite at the left margin below | 5.1(a) |
| A 62-word quotation block-quoted inside `(...)` | Keep it inline in quotation marks | 5.1(a) |

## Typst Rendering

Write the spaced periods **literally** in the `.typ` source — `. . .` and `. . . .`. Typst has no
Bluebook-aware ellipsis; whatever you type is what renders.

```typst
#quote(block: true)[
  Although perfection is not possible, or expected, the result here could have been
  entirely different if UOP had appointed an independent negotiating committee . . .
]
```

This matches the house style already in use: `~/areas/corps/slides/12-mergers-acquisitions.typ`
writes judicial quotations with spaced periods, including the four-dot form (`"...unless actual
coercion or disclosure violations are shown. . . . In short, ..."`). Note that the same file uses
an unspaced `...` in its news-headline cards — journalism excerpts, not Bluebook-governed
quotations. Do not copy that form into a footnote.

To keep the dots from breaking across a line (the VLR Slatebook requires non-breaking spaces
throughout the ellipsis), use Typst's non-breaking space `~`: `barrier~.~.~.~now`. The corps
slides do not do this; plain spaces are acceptable where line-breaking is not a concern.

## Verification Checklist

Before finalizing any quotation:

- [ ] Quotation is verbatim — every word checked against the source, not memory
- [ ] 50+ words → block format; 49 or fewer → quotation marks
- [ ] Block quote carries no enclosing quotation marks; citation starts at the left margin
- [ ] Block quote's introducing clause is complete-with-colon or incomplete-without
- [ ] No quotation begins with an ellipsis
- [ ] Every ellipsis is three spaced periods, set off by a space on each side
- [ ] End-of-sentence omissions carry the fourth period (the sentence's own)
- [ ] No space before the dots when the retained period ends the original sentence
- [ ] Deletions after the last quoted sentence's final punctuation are left unmarked
- [ ] Every bracket marks a real change; no bracket sits on an unchanged word
- [ ] `[sic]` used only where a bracket cannot fix the error
- [ ] Omitted citations/footnotes carry a parenthetical, not an ellipsis
- [ ] No `(emphasis in original)` anywhere
- [ ] Alteration parentheticals are separate, and ordered per Rule 1.5(b)
