## The Iron Law of Verbatim Quotes

**WHEN THE SOURCE CONTAINS A DIRECT QUOTE, PRESERVE IT VERBATIM. This is not negotiable.**

Statutory text, judicial language, and direct quotes from the paper are precise — every word matters. When the source paper, statute, or opinion contains a direct quote, it must appear in the slides **exactly as it appears in the source**.

**Do NOT:**
- Paraphrase statutory language
- Truncate quotes with ellipsis unless the source itself uses ellipsis
- Summarize a quote into your own words
- "Clean up" archaic or awkward phrasing
- Reconstruct quotes from training knowledge

**Do:**
- Copy the full quote verbatim from the source
- Preserve original formatting (italics, emphasis) where Typst supports it
- Present quotes as plain text within bullets or as `#quote()` blocks — NOT in `#callout()` boxes
- If the quote causes overflow, fix by adjusting surrounding content — never by trimming the quote
- When a quote is too long for one slide, split at a natural sentence boundary

**Why:** Statutory text IS the law. Opinion quotes are what the court actually said. Paper quotes are what the authors actually wrote. Paraphrasing changes meaning. Training knowledge reconstructs quotes with subtle errors.
</EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>
