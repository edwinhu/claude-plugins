## Rule

When in doubt about whether to skip a step, ask: **"If I skip this, does the user's published work get worse?"**

If yes, do not skip. The user experiences the quality of the final document, not your sense of completion. Every step you skip to "help faster" chooses YOUR comfort over the USER's outcome.

## Rationale

**Why this exists** — agents are driven by multiple motivations: helpfulness, competence, efficiency, approval, honesty. When these conflict, efficiency often wins because it feels productive. But the user doesn't experience your efficiency — they experience the quality of their published document. Skipping a review step saves you 2 minutes but costs the user a published error. This constraint reframes every shortcut decision around the user's actual outcome.

## Examples

### Correct
1. Agent considers skipping outline review because "the outline looks good." Asks: "If I skip this, does the user's published work get worse?" Answer: Yes, unreviewed outlines may have structural gaps that propagate to drafts. → Does not skip.
2. Agent considers skipping ai-anti-patterns loading for a "quick fix." Asks the question. Answer: Yes, the fix may introduce AI writing smell. → Loads anti-patterns.

### Incorrect
1. Agent skips the validation phase because "the draft covers everything." Published work has a missing claim.
2. Agent skips constraint loading because "it's just one sentence." Published sentence uses hollow hedging.

## Rationalization Table

| Excuse | Reality | Do Instead |
|--------|---------|------------|
| "This step is redundant" | You think it's redundant because you haven't done it yet. It may catch something. | Do the step. |
| "I'm saving the user time" | You're saving YOUR time. The user pays the cost of skipped quality. | Do the step. |
| "The user won't notice" | The user's readers will notice. Reviewers will notice. | Do the step. |
| "I can always fix it later" | Later = never. Each phase builds on the previous. Errors compound. | Do the step now. |

## Red Flags

- **"This step seems unnecessary"** → STOP. Ask the drive-aligned question: does the user's published work get worse if you skip it?
- **"I'm being efficient by skipping this"** → STOP. Efficient for whom? The user experiences quality, not your speed.
- **"The user just wants it done"** → STOP. The user wants it done RIGHT. They invoked a structured workflow for a reason.
- **"It's good enough"** → STOP. "Good enough" is your assessment, not the user's. Follow the process.
